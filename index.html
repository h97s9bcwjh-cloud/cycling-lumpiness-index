Cycling Lumpiness Index Beta

Cycling Lumpiness Index (CLI) v3.0 is a candidate 0–10 measure of how “lumpy” a cycling route is. It is designed to capture repeated changes in gradient and the way a road alternates between climbing and descending, rather than simply how much elevation a ride gains. A long steady mountain climb can therefore have a low CLI, while a rolling route with repeated ramps, dips and short descents can score highly.

How is CLI calculated?

Cycling Lumpiness Index (CLI) v3.0 uses the route elevation profile resampled at 25 m intervals. It combines a multi-scale Haar-style wavelet measure of gradient roughness with meaningful climb↔descent reversal densities at 250–500 m and 2–4 km scales.

CLI = min(10, 0.53·P50 + 6.58·Rshort + 18.16·Rlong)

A ±2% neutral gradient band is used in the reversal analysis to avoid counting tiny fluctuations around flat.

Reproducibility note: v3.0 uses exact 25 m spatial resampling and an exact n-point boxcar smoother for reversal detection, with zero-padding at route boundaries. These discrete rules reproduce the development implementation used to generate the frozen v3.0 scores.

The graphics relate these terms back to the route: the teal P(x) trace shows local wavelet-derived gradient roughness along the ride; the dashed line is its median P50. Yellow dots mark short/medium climb↔descent reversals, while larger red dots mark long-scale reversals.

About the CLI

Cycling Lumpiness Index (CLI) v3.0 is a candidate 0–10 measure of how “lumpy” a cycling route is. It is designed to distinguish repeated changes in gradient and climbing/descending structure from simple elevation gain or steepness. A long steady climb can therefore have a low CLI, while a rolling sawtooth route can score highly.

The calculation combines multi-scale gradient roughness with short/medium (roughly 250–500 m) and longer (roughly 2–4 km) climb–descent reversal structure. The current whole-ride formula was frozen before two blinded validation batches totalling 24 rides; against one experienced rider’s subjective ratings it achieved a mean absolute error of 0.73/10 and Spearman rank correlation of 0.941.

Ride summary: distance, minimum/maximum elevation and total elevation gain are calculated from the same distance-normalised elevation profile used by CLI. Elevation gain is the sum of positive elevation changes after 25 m spatial resampling.

Privacy: GPX processing takes place in your browser. The calculator does not need to send the ride file to a server. If you submit a rider rating, the site stores the rating together with derived CLI statistics, a one-way ride fingerprint, and an anonymised 25 m distance–elevation profile. It does not store your GPX file, latitude/longitude coordinates, filename, name, email address or Strava athlete ID.

Beta status: CLI v3.0 is experimental and remains subject to wider validation. It is not a navigation, safety or route-suitability tool.
